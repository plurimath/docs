== Structure of a formula

The root rule is `expression`.

* An expression is one or more terms in a row. `{a \over b}` and `a \over b` divide the terms before `\over` by the terms after it (captures `dividend` and `divisor`).
* A term (rule `sequence`) is one of the constructs below. When none of the special forms applies, it is an operand, optionally with scripts.
* An operand (rule `intermediate_exp`) is a single symbol, number or command, a group in braces `{...}`, or a bracketed group: an opening bracket, an optional expression and a closing bracket. The closing bracket may be missing; the model then records it as absent.

=== Scripts

`_` gives a subscript and `^` a superscript. Both may be given, in either order. A script is one operand: one letter, one number, one command or one group. So `x^ab` is `x` with superscript `a`, followed by `b`, while `x^10` has the superscript `10`, because a digit string is one number.

.Scripts and their model classes
[cols="2,3", options="headers"]
|===
|Form |Model

|`a_b` |`Base` with `subscript`
|`a^b` |`Power` with `supscript`
|`a_b^c`, `a^c_b` |`PowerBase` with `subscript` and `supscript`
|===

Large operators `\sum`, `\prod`, `\int` and `\oint` take their limits as scripts and the operand after them as `third_value` (class `TernaryClass`). A function name followed by `\limits`, such as `\max\limits_{i}`, is the `limits` form.

=== Fractions and other two-argument commands

These commands take two arguments (capture `binary`): `\frac`, `\binom`, `\overset`, `\underset`, `\stackrel`, `\color`, and also `\lim`, `\log` and `\inf`, which take their scripts as arguments. `\sqrt{x}` is a square root; `\sqrt[n]{x}` takes the index in brackets as `first_value`. `\rule` takes up to three optional arguments. `\bmod`, `\pmod` and `\mod` stand between two operands (capture `under_over`).

=== One-argument commands

Function names and accents with one argument (capture `unary_functions`):

`\arccos \arcsin \arctan \bar \cancel \cos \cosh \cot \coth \csc \ddot \deg \det \dim \dot \exp \gcd \hat \hom \ker \lg \liminf \limsup \ln \max \min \overline \phantom \sec \sin \sinh \sup \tan \tanh \tilde \underbrace \vec`

The argument may be omitted, may be a bracketed operand or may be a `\left ... \right` group. Each of these may also carry scripts.

`\overbrace` takes its argument and the label above it (capture `underover`).

=== Fonts and text

Font commands take one argument. The model keeps the command name as the text field `fonts` next to the argument:

`\Bbb \bf \cal \displaystyle \mathbb \mathbf \mathbfit \mathbold \mathcal \mathds \mathfrak \mathit \mathrm \mathsf \mathsfbf \mathsfbfit \mathsfit \mathtt \mbf \mbfit \mbfitsans \mbfsans \mfrak \mit \msans \mscr \mtt \rm \textbf \textit \textrm`

`\text{...}` and `\mbox{...}` hold literal text, kept as the caller wrote it: the field `text` names the command and `first_value` holds the text. `\operatorname{...}` holds the name of an operator.

=== Brackets

`\left` and `\right` enclose a group (model class `LeftRight`). The bracket after `\left` or `\right` may be `.` or missing. The grammar accepts `\left( x` with no `\right`. Recognised brackets include `(`, `)`, `[`, `]`, `\{`, `\}`, `\lbrace`, `\rbrace`, `\lbrack`, `\rbrack`, `\langle`, `\rangle`, `\lfloor`, `\rfloor`, `\lceil`, `\rceil`, `|`, `\vert`, `\Vert`, `\|` and `\backslash`, together with their Unicode characters.

=== Environments

`\begin{name} ... \end{name}` reads an environment whose rows are separated by `\\` (attribute `row_break`) and whose cells are separated by `&`. A starred name and an optional argument in brackets are accepted. `\begin{array}{...}` takes its column specification as `args`. The grammar also has a command form, `\matrix{...}` and the like, for the names `matrix`, `pmatrix`, `bmatrix`, `Bmatrix`, `vmatrix`, `Vmatrix`, `align`, `split`, `multline` and `array`.

The grammar does not check that the name after `\end` matches the name after `\begin`. The typed model does not build rows and cells: the body of the environment is the same nested expression as any other formula, and `&` and `\\` appear in it as an `operant` and a `row_break` (Annex A). `\substack` takes one operand.

=== Symbols and numbers

* A single ASCII letter is a symbol.
* A command name from the symbol table, such as `\alpha` or `\leq`, is a symbol. The table, `data/latex-symbols.json` of the `latexmath` gem, has 3,280 spellings at the pinned commit. A character reference such as `&#x3b1;` is also looked up there.
* `"` is a literal symbol, and the ASCII punctuation `! # $ % & ' * + , - . / : ; < = > ? @ ^ _ |` reads as operators.
* Numbers are digit strings, with an optional decimal part: `1`, `1.5`, `.5`. Hexadecimal (`0x1F`), binary (`0b101`) and octal (`0o17`) numbers are separate forms.
* `\ ` is a space and `\;` a thick space.

Each symbol in the model is a `MathSymbol` with three fields: `spelling` (the text matched), `value` (its Unicode text from the table) and `kind` (`symbol`, `open` or `close`). A spelling the table lacks stands for itself.
