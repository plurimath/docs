== Exact-decimal discipline

=== The grammar

Every number the node carries is a plain decimal:

[cols="1,2,3", options="headers"]
.Number grammar
|===
|Field |Grammar |Examples

|value |`-? digits ( . digits )?` |`10.3`, `-42`, `0.0015`
|magnitude, coverage_factor, coverage_probability, lower, upper |`digits ( . digits )?` |`0.2`, `2`, `0.95`
|===

No exponent forms, no signs on the unsigned fields, no separators. A value carried as anything else — a computed float, a `Number` with a base or a size qualifier, an empty string — is refused at construction.

=== Verbatim preservation

The node stores the digits as written. `2.10` and `2.1` are different exact decimals: the trailing zero is the author's statement of precision, and the model does not speak over it. Every encoding emits the digits verbatim; every exact comparison in this model — the concise rule, the probability bounds — compares the decimal strings digit-wise without converting to floating point.

[example]
A node with value `10.30`, magnitude `0.20`.
*Expected outcome.* every encoding emits `10.30` and `0.20` with the trailing zeros intact; no surface normalises them away.

=== Rounding is the author's act

The node never calculates, rounds or converts its numbers (JCGM 100 7.2.2). Where evaluation (document 1004) produces numbers, they are produced as exact decimals by the evaluation rules of UnitsML 2 and attached to new nodes; an existing node's fields are never rewritten in place.

=== Comparisons without floating point

The two comparisons the model performs — the concise decimal-places rule and the coverage-probability bounds — are defined on the digit strings:

* the probability check requires the first nonzero digit of the fraction to exist (strictly greater than zero) and the string to compare strictly less than `"1"` digit-wise;
* the concise rule compares decimal-place counts, which is a property of the strings alone.

Both refuse inputs whose comparison would require rounding.

=== Worked comparisons

The comparisons are on strings, and the examples below pin the edge behaviour.

[example]
Probability `0.999` against the bound `1`.
*Expected outcome.* accepted: digit-wise, `0.999` is strictly less than `1`. The check does not convert to float; a float conversion of `0.999999999999999999` would equal `1.0` and wrongly refuse.

[example]
Probability `1.0`.
*Expected outcome.* refused: the string equals the upper bound digit-wise; a probability of exactly one is not a coverage probability.

[example]
Probability `0.0`.
*Expected outcome.* refused: the string has no nonzero digit, so it is not strictly greater than zero.

[example]
Probability `0.90`.
*Expected outcome.* accepted: trailing zeros do not affect the digit-wise comparison; the encoding emits `0.90` verbatim, never `0.9`.

[example]
Magnitude `0.2` against value `10.3` for conciseness: decimal places 1 and 1.
*Expected outcome.* accepted; the concise digit string is `2`.

[example]
Magnitude `0.02` against value `10.3` for conciseness: decimal places 2 and 1.
*Expected outcome.* refused: the magnitudes' decimal places do not match the value's, and `10.3(02)` is not a defined form.

=== Where the discipline comes from

JCGM 100 7.2.2 fixes the reporting forms and assigns rounding to the reporter. The exact-decimal discipline is that assignment made executable: the model cannot round because it does not compute, and it cannot silently normalise because its numbers are text. The same discipline governs the whole Plurimath stack through UnitsML 2; this document applies it to the uncertainty fields.
