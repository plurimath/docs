== The reading algorithm

=== The stages

The reader proceeds in four stages:

. **Tokenise.** The input splits into tokens by longest match against the constants: construct names and fonts first (longest name wins), then symbol tokens, then numbers, then structure characters, then fences. A character sequence matching nothing is a reading error with its position.
. **Resolve.** Tokens resolve through the constants' classification: unary, binary, ternary, sub/sup, font, special alphabet, symbol, number, fence, structure.
. **Build.** The construct tree builds by the precedence of clause 13: classes take their arguments (parenthesised or adjacent), scripts attach, fractions bind, operators fold.
. **Validate.** Uncertainty nodes construct through PM 1002's validation; the tree yields.

The stages are separable only in description; the implementation interleaves them. What this document pins is the outcome: the tree a given input yields, and the error a malformed input raises.

=== Longest match

Longest match is the tokeniser's rule and the disambiguator: `==> ` is one arrow; `<=` is one relation; `(:` opens an angle fence; `ZZ` is the number-set token before `Z` could be a symbol. Construct names match before symbols share their letters — `sum` is the nary construct even though a symbol could begin with `s`.

=== The fences

Fences pair per Annex C's table. The pairing is by token: an opener matches its own closer, and nesting tracks the open stack. The unpaired cases — an opener without its closer, a closer without an opener — are reading errors naming the fence and its position.

=== Errors

The reading errors are PM 1003's, and this notation produces them thus:

=== What the reader never does

It never guesses an intent for a malformed input, never skips a faulting token to continue, never normalises digits, never resolves a symbol outside the registry, and never returns a partially built tree. A reading is total or absent.

=== A worked reading

`uexp(10.3, 0.5, 2, 0.95) "unitsml(g)" + x`:

. tokenise: the construct name, its fence, the number tokens, the channel string, the plus, the name;
. resolve: `uexp` is a construct with its fixed arity; the channel matches;
. build: the call takes four arguments; the channel is the unit operand following per PM 1002's placement; the addition joins the uncertainty node and `x` as operands;
. validate: the uncertainty node constructs through PM 1002 — category expanded, fields complete, factor positive, probability in range.

*Expected outcome.* an addition of an expanded uncertainty (with unit) and a name — reading total, tree valid, ready for any writer.

=== Positional accuracy of errors

Every error of clause 14 carries its input position: the tokeniser's offsets survive into the typed errors, so a caller can point at the offending character. The corpus pins the positions for its error vectors — a refusal without a position is not a conforming refusal.

=== Tokenisation's edge cases, pinned

* `(:` opens an angle fence only where a fence can open — inside a construct argument it is still a fence; the construct's argument grammar wraps it.
* `((` is two opens, not one special token; the table recognition of clause 9 happens at the construct-argument level.
* `2.10` is one number; `2.1.0` is a number then a fault; `..` is two faults in a row and the reader reports the first.
* The reader is Unicode-native: symbol tokens are Unicode where the registry says so, and the letter-pair fences are ASCII. A lookalike character (a fullwidth parenthesis for `(`) is an unresolvable run, named and positioned.

=== Recovery is not a feature

The reader stops at the first fault. A specification of recovery — skip, resync, best-effort — is a specification of a second reader with different guarantees; this document specifies one reader with complete guarantees instead. Applications that want fault tolerance get it by splitting input and reading the pieces; the typed errors with positions make that practical.

=== The reader against the writer

The reader of this document and the writer of PM 1001's AsciiMath surface are one interface's two halves (PM 1003). The reader is the generous half — the surface is what it accepts, tokens, synonyms and all. The writer is the conservative half — canonical tokens, canonical forms, one encoding per construct. An author may write generously; a writer never does. Round trips pass through both halves and yield equal trees with re-spelled tokens where synonyms were written — the tree is the same even when the text is not.

=== The reading algorithm, recited

Tokenise by longest match; resolve through the constants' classification; build by the smallest-operand precedence with grouping as the override; validate the uncertainty nodes through PM 1002; yield the tree or the typed error with its position. Four stages, one pass, no second chances — the reader's entire behaviour is this recitation plus the tables, and the corpus is the proof that the recitation is complete.
