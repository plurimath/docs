== Scripts and fractions

=== Scripts

`^` attaches a superscript and `_` a subscript to the preceding operand: `x^2`, `x_1`, `x_1^2`. The operand is the preceding group — a token, a parenthesised expression, or a construct's result — and the script content is the following group. Both orders of sub and superscript on one operand read to `PowerBase`; a lone script reads to `Power` or `Base`.

[example]
`x^2`, `x_1`, `x_1^2`, `x^2_1`.
*Expected outcome.* the four readings: `Power`, `Base`, and two `PowerBase` nodes that are equal — the script order on one operand is not significant.

=== Scripts on constructs

Scripts attach to the construct that just closed: `sum_(i=1)^n`, `lim_(x->0)`, `log_2 x`. The sub/sup classes read the scripts as their limits; the ternary classes read them as the nary limits.

=== Fractions

`a/b` reads to `Frac`: the operand before the slash is the numerator, the operand after is the denominator, each being a token or a group. `((n(n+1))/2)` shows the idiom — groups on both sides of the slash. The slash binds tighter than addition and looser than scripts: `a/b^2` is a over `b²`, and `a^2/b` is `a²` over `b`.

=== Precedence interplay

The full precedence is clause 13; this clause states the local rules: scripts bind tightest, then fences and constructs, then fractions, then adjacency, then the operators. The reader's behaviour is the definition; the corpus pins the contested shapes vector by vector.

=== The fraction edge cases

`a//b` — the double slash — is not a token; the reader sees two slashes and errors. `a/b/c` is `(a/b)/c` — fractions chain left-associatively. `a/b^2` puts the script inside the denominator: scripts bind tighter than the slash.

=== Scripts on scripts

A script's content may carry scripts: `x^(y_1)` nests the base inside the exponent. Nesting is unlimited and the tree carries it as written — the parser's levels describe sibling precedence, not a depth limit. The corpus carries the two-deep and three-deep cases.

=== The empty script

`x^()` — a script with an empty group — reads to a script node with an empty operand: the `None` node fills the slot, the same construct MathML uses for the empty half of a pair. The author who means "nothing here yet" has a notation; the corpus pins it.
