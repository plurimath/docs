== Annex C: the fences

=== The fence table

The reader's parenthesis table, normative:

.Fences
[cols="2,2,3", options="headers"]
|===
|Opener |Closer |Content reading

|`(` |`)` |ordinary grouping and construct arguments
|`[` |`]` |bracket grouping; row delimiters inside table grids
|`{` |`}` |brace grouping; brace-delimited matrices
|`ᑕ` |`ᑐ` |the single-glyph bracket pair, content as ordinary
|`ℒ` |`ℛ` |the script-letter bracket pair, content as ordinary
|`(:` |`:)` |the angle-bracket pair of the public definition
|bar pair |itself |the bar pair — `Vmatrix` delimiters and absolute-value-like grouping per context
|===

The multi-glyph pairs (`ᑕ`/`ᑐ`, `ℒ`/`ℛ`) and the letter pair `(:`/`:)` are Plurimath's additions to the public set; the plain three are the baseline's.

*Compatibility note.* The public definition also carries the bare `:` closer; Plurimath's pairing is strictly two-sided per this table, and a bare closer is a reading error.

=== Nesting

Fences nest: `((a),(b))` is a parenthesised group containing a comma-adjacent pair — the table-reader sees the grid only in the bracketed forms of clause 9. The open stack tracks pairs by opener identity; the table forms are recognised at their own positions, not by the fence stack.

=== Fences as constructs

`abs(x)`, `norm(x)`, `floor(x)`, `ceil(x)`, `obrace(x)`, `ubrace(x)` are unary constructs, not bare fences: their delimiters come from the construct, and the bare fence characters group without a node of their own. The distinction matters for round trips: a construct's fence survives (the construct carries it), a bare group's fence survives as grouping.

=== The pairs in use

.Worked fence cases
[cols="2,4", options="headers"]
|===
|Input |Reading

|`(:a, b:)` |the angle fence around the pair — the public definition's inner product form
|`ᑕaᑐ` |the single-glyph bracket pair, content ordinary
|`ℒxℛ` |the script-letter pair, content ordinary
|bar-x-bar |the bar pair grouping — or `abs x`, which carries its fence on the node
|`((a),(b))` |two opens — adjacency, not a table; the grid reading needs the bracketed form
|===

The bar pair's dual role (grouping here, `Vmatrix` delimiter there) is resolved by position: around a bracketed grid it delimits the matrix, elsewhere it groups. The corpus carries both positions.

=== Fences and round trips

A bare group's fence is grouping, and grouping is not a node — so a round trip may re-spell the grouping (parentheses for brackets where the tree allows). The construct fences (`abs`, `norm`, and kin) ride their nodes and survive exactly. An author who needs a specific fence in the output uses the construct that owns it; an author who needs only grouping takes the round trip's re-spelling. The corpus freezes which cases re-spell and which do not.
